Definition · Concept

Concept Data mining is the extraction of patterns from large data that are non-trivial, previously unknown and potentially useful.

the same supermarket file, used two ways

Non-trivial

The result needs a search or a model. A look-up or a single sum does not count.

Previously unknown

The pattern was not written into the question. The analyst asked what goes together and did not name butter or jam.

Potentially useful

Somebody can act on it: a shelf plan, a credit decision, a maintenance visit.

KDD process · Concept

Concept Knowledge discovery in databases (KDD) is a seven-step process, and data mining in the narrow sense is its fifth step.

Han, Kamber & Pei, figure 1.4

Steps 1 to 4 prepare the data

Cleaning, integration, selection and transformation turn recorded data into a table a method can read.

Step 5 runs the method

Association rules, a classifier, a clustering. Textbooks on algorithms cover this step.

Steps 6 and 7 filter and report

A run returns far more patterns than a person can read. The last two steps decide which ones reach the user.

KDD process · Example: step 1, cleaning

Example continued 1 Cleaning found four kinds of defect: duplicate rows, missing values, impossible values and inconsistent spellings.

exact duplicate sales rows179
missing amounts240
impossible ages23
city spellings7 → 4
customers.csv · city, as typed
Chennai is spelled four ways. Merged, it has 647 + 60 + 50 + 40 = 797 customers.
Duplicates. 179 rows repeat another row in every column. Dropping them leaves 40,031 rows.
Missing amounts. Each item has a single price in the file, so the 240 missing amounts were filled as quantity × price.
Ages. 9 customers have age −1 and 14 have an age above 120, up to 210. These were set to missing.
KDD process · Example: step 2, integration

Example continued 2 Integration joins the sales rows to the customer file. 300 of the 12,000 baskets carry a customer id that the customer file does not have.

left join · sales ← customers
What the join adds. Each sales row now carries the age, city and joining year of the customer: 10 columns where there were 7.
What it exposes. The 300 unmatched baskets have ids that start with W. They are walk-in sales given a temporary id at the till. They stay in the basket analysis and drop out of anything that needs age or city.
KDD process · Example: steps 3 and 4, selection and transformation

Example continued 3 Selection keeps four columns. Transformation turns 40,031 item rows into a table of 12,000 baskets by 14 items.

ten of the 12,000 baskets
A basket holds 3.34 different items on average and at most 10.
Selection. Kept: txn_id, date, hour, item. Dropped for this task: cust_id, qty, amount, age, city, member_since.
Transformation. Quantity is reduced to bought or not bought. A basket with three loaves counts as bread once.
KDD process · Example: step 5, data mining

Example continued 4 The mining step counts how many baskets contain each item, pair and triple, and writes every rule X → y those counts allow.

baskets containing each item, of 12,000
items14
itemsets counted469
rules written1,274
Itemsets. 14 single items, 91 pairs and 364 triples make 469 counts.
Rules. A pair gives two rules and a triple gives three with one item on the right, so 91 × 2 + 364 × 3 = 1,274 rules.
At this point the output is a list of 1,274 lines. Which of them matter is the next step.
KDD process · Example: step 6, pattern evaluation

Example continued 5 Three thresholds cut 1,274 rules to 22. The three measures are defined in the next section.

rules left after each threshold
Support removes rules seen in too few baskets to trust: 636 go.
Confidence and lift remove rules that hold too seldom, and rules whose right side is common anyway: 616 more go.
KDD process · Example: step 7, knowledge presentation

Example continued 6 The first of the 22 surviving rules, written for the person who decides shelf plans and promotions.

share of baskets that contain jam
A basket with butter is 3.4 times as likely to contain jam as a basket picked at random.
What the manager reads. Six in ten customers who buy butter also buy jam. The two are shelved in different aisles.
What was left out. Support, confidence and lift do not appear in the sentence. The chart and the count behind each bar carry them.
KDD process · Mechanism

Mechanism A data mining system puts the seven steps into five connected parts, with a person steering the search.

parts of a data mining system
The loop is the normal case. A first run shows a rule, the user asks whether it holds on weekdays, and the engine runs again on a subset.
The knowledge base holds what the data does not. That butter and jam are both breakfast items is known to the buyer and is recorded in no column.
KDD process · Drawbacks

Drawbacks Each step of the walk-through made a choice that a different analyst could have made differently, and the result depends on those choices.

The fill rule was checked first

The 240 missing amounts were filled as quantity × price because every item has one price in this file. With discounts in the data that fill would be wrong.

The join decides who is counted

300 walk-in baskets have no age or city. Any result by city describes 11,700 baskets and says so, or it misleads.

The thresholds were set by hand

1% support, 50% confidence and 1.2 lift left 22 rules. Other values leave a different list, and the data does not say which values are right.

A report of a mining result lists the choices made in steps 1 to 4 next to the pattern.
Interestingness · Concept

Concept Three counts on ten baskets give the three measures of the rule butter → jam.

ten baskets from the file · butter in blue, jam in red

Support = 3 / 10 = 0.30

The share of all baskets that contain butter and jam together. 3 of the ten rows show both colours.

Confidence = 3 / 4 = 0.75

Among the 4 baskets with butter, the share that also contain jam.

Lift = 0.75 / 0.40 = 1.88

Confidence divided by the share of all baskets with jam, which is 4 / 10. A lift of 1 means butter tells you nothing about jam.

Interestingness · Test

Test The rule milk → bread has a confidence of 62.1%, higher than butter → jam. Its lift is 0.97.

share of baskets that contain bread
Bread is in 63.7% of all baskets. Knowing that a basket has milk moves that to 62.1%.
The test. Compare the confidence of X → y with the share of all baskets that contain y. Their ratio is the lift.
The result here. A lift of 0.97 says milk and bread are bought independently of each other. The rule is true and carries no information.

Confidence rewards a common right side. Bread is in 63.7% of baskets, so almost any left side gives a confidence near that figure. 77 of the 103 rules that pass 1% support and 50% confidence end in bread.
Lift has its own weakness. It is unstable for rare items: a pair seen in three baskets can show a lift of 20. Support guards against that.
Used together. Support for trust, confidence for strength, lift for surprise. Whether a surviving rule is new to the business is a question for the people who run it.
Interestingness · Reveal

Reveal Nappies → beer has a confidence of 22.3% over the whole file. Split by time of purchase, it is 59.7% on Friday evenings and 6.8% otherwise.

share of nappy baskets that also contain beer
Outside Friday evening the lift is 0.96: no association. The whole-file lift of 2.76 comes from 822 of the 12,000 baskets.
A pattern can belong to a subset. Mining the same rule along day, hour, store or customer group is called multidimensional mining.
It depended on step 3. The split was possible because selection kept the date and hour columns. Had they been dropped, the file would show a weak rule and no reason for it.
Where the field comes from

Fields that data mining draws on Each method in this course was developed in one of six older fields. Data mining combines them to work on data that was recorded for another purpose.

Han, Kamber & Pei, section 1.5
What is specific to data mining. The data is recorded before there is a question for it: a till records sales to bill customers, and the analysis is thought of afterwards. Size, defects and mixed data types are therefore part of every project.
Where the field comes from

Five readings of the supermarket file The same file answers a different kind of question in each neighbouring field.

The first four name their target. Butter, city, the pair butter and jam, the column jam: each was chosen by the analyst before the run.
The fifth leaves it open. The run searches all pairs and triples. It then borrows the statistician's test and the database's counting to do so.

Challenges · Cost

Cost With d items there are 2d − 1 itemsets and 3d − 2d+1 + 1 rules. Fourteen items give 16,383 and 4,750,202.

possible rules (upper bar) and itemsets (lower bar) for d items · log scale
This file. Of the 16,383 possible itemsets, 6,274 occur in at least one basket.
A real shop. A supermarket stocks thousands of items. At 100 items the count of itemsets is already 1.27 × 1030.
What methods do about it. They skip itemsets that cannot reach the support threshold, sample, or run in parallel.
Challenges · Society

Mining personal data After step 2 the file links 11,700 baskets to a named customer record, and a rule about items becomes a statement about people.

what the integrated file allows

Privacy

Customers gave their details for a loyalty card. In India the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 governs what else that data may be used for.

Fairness

A model trained on past decisions repeats them. A credit model learns from loans that earlier officers chose to approve.

Invisible mining

Search ranking, recommendations and advertising run these methods on every user, without a separate notice each time.
